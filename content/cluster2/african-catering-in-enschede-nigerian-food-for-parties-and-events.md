## What to Include in Your Catering Menu

Building a Nigerian catering menu is an art. You want a balance of universally loved staples, adventurous traditional dishes, and crowd-pleasing snacks. Here is a guide to the perfect West African spread for your event in Enschede.

### The Centerpiece: Party Jollof Rice
You cannot host a Nigerian-themed event without Jollof rice. It is the absolute centerpiece of the table. When catered in large, steaming trays, Jollof rice looks vibrant and smells incredible. The catering version, often referred to as "party Jollof," is specifically cooked to achieve that signature smoky flavor that cannot easily be replicated in small batches at home. If you want the best "Jollof rijst Enschede" has to offer at your party, Mama DD's provides exactly that.

### The Meats: Suya, Asun, and Fried Chicken
Nigerian parties are heavily protein-focused. A diverse selection of meats is essential:
- **Fried/Grilled Chicken**: A universal crowd-pleaser that pairs perfectly with Jollof rice.
- **Suya**: If you want to impress, offer trays of Suya. These are thin skewers of beef or chicken, generously coated in a spicy, nutty peanut rub (Yaji) and grilled. It is the ultimate party food.
- **Asun**: For the bold eaters, Asun—spicy, roasted, bite-sized goat meat tossed in a fiery pepper sauce—will be a massive hit. It is incredibly rich and deeply flavorful.

### The Traditional Touch: Soups and Swallows
To provide a truly authentic experience, you should include a traditional soup and swallow option. This is where the real cultural immersion happens.
- **Egusi Soup**: A rich, hearty stew made from ground melon seeds, spinach, and palm oil. It is very approachable for first-timers.
- **Pounded Yam**: The most popular swallow to pair with Egusi. It is smooth, slightly sweet, and visually striking when served in neat, round mounds.

### The Essential Sides: Dodo and Moi Moi
A Nigerian buffet is incomplete without the proper accompaniments.
- **Dodo (Fried Plantains)**: Sweet, golden-brown fried plantains are an absolute necessity. They provide the perfect sweet counterbalance to the savory, spicy main dishes. Be warned: trays of Dodo disappear incredibly fast!
- **Moi Moi**: Savory steamed bean puddings made from black-eyed peas, onions, and peppers. They add a wonderful, soft texture and a great vegetarian protein option to the spread.

### The Starters and Snacks: Meat Pies and Puff-Puff
For a more casual event or a standing reception, Nigerian snacks are perfect.
- **Meat Pies**: Flaky, buttery pastries filled with seasoned minced meat and potatoes. They are hearty, easy to eat while mingling, and universally loved.
- **Puff-Puff**: The ultimate party dessert. These deep-fried, sweet dough balls are soft, airy, and dangerously addictive. Think of them as the life of the party!

## Accommodating Dietary Preferences

One of the great advantages of Nigerian cuisine is its natural versatility. When planning an event, you inevitably have to consider various dietary restrictions and preferences among your guests. 

For vegetarian and vegan attendees, a Nigerian spread offers far more than just a boring salad. Dishes like Jollof rice (when prepared with vegetable broth), sweet fried plantains (Dodo), and Moi Moi (steamed bean pudding) are naturally plant-based and incredibly filling. Furthermore, many traditional soups can be customized to feature an abundance of leafy greens, mushrooms, and locust beans, entirely omitting the meat while retaining the deep, complex flavors.
